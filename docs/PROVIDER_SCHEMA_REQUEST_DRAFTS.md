## Shirley Ryan AbilityLab wearable-sensor program

Subject: Schema request for independent wearable gait-model evaluation

Dear Dr Jayaraman and study team,

We are developing a research model that distinguishes available healthy and
post-stroke walking cohorts using lower-back acceleration. Its specificity
against other gait disorders remains limited, so we are seeking independent,
clinically characterized data rather than claiming diagnostic readiness.

Could your team share a de-identified data dictionary or small schema example
for NCT04219670, if data sharing is feasible? We first need to establish whether
healthy and stroke participants have comparable walking recordings, raw or
losslessly reconstructable signals, documented lower-back and bilateral-foot
placements, native sampling rates/units/channel order, and participant/trial IDs.

Please also indicate available age, sex, stroke severity/chronicity, walking
speed and aid metadata; participant counts; any non-stroke comparison groups;
potential overlap with other releases; and conditions for external evaluation
and publication. If the current acquisition differs, we would welcome discussing
a prospective compatible protocol. We are not requesting a full archive now.

## Santa Lucia IRCCS gait research team

Subject: Gait-data schema and clinical specificity evaluation inquiry

Dear Dr Tramontano and study team,

We are preparing an independent evaluation of a lower-back wearable gait research
model. A key unresolved problem is positive stroke scores in non-stroke gait
disorders, making clinically identified comparison groups particularly valuable.

Could you clarify whether the data associated with NCT04691102 can support
controlled external evaluation, starting with a de-identified dictionary or
schema sample? In particular, we need exact sensor placements: a sacral/trunk
description does not by itself establish the lower-back and bilateral-foot
contract in our protocol. Please identify whether such channels exist or whether
a prospective compatible acquisition would be necessary.

We would also appreciate raw-signal availability, synchronization, native units,
sampling rates, walking annotations, counts by healthy/stroke/non-stroke group,
clinical metadata and missingness, possible overlap with related published
cohorts, and applicable data-use/publication conditions. We are requesting schema
information first, not an archive or permission to make clinical claims.
