# Stroke-like synthesis: parameter specification v0.1

2026-09-09. First evidence-backed specification and executable consistency checks.
No stroke motion or IMU signals have been generated by this work. Clinical ranges,
full-body dynamics, lower-back attachment and independent validation remain open.

## Primary evidence checked

[Balasubramanian et al., 2007, full author-hosted paper](https://sites.utexas.edu/neptune/files/2020/07/apmr881.pdf)
studied 49 people with chronic hemiparesis at self-selected walking speeds.
Their step-length ratio is paretic/nonparetic. Figure 2 illustrates paired
ratio/speed observations: 1.47 at .71 m/s, .98 at .75 m/s, and .68 at .52 m/s.
These are individual examples, not severity ranges or a distribution to sample.
Both asymmetry directions occur. The relationship with paretic propulsion does
not justify deriving force from ratio alone. Brunnstrom grouping in this paper
must not be relabeled as FAC. These observations support retaining signed side
information and joint profiles rather than universally shortening a paretic step.

This is a bounded first source review, not a comprehensive clinical synthesis.
Ms. Chua's responses are unverified; no clinician-supplied values are used.

## Parameter contract

P denotes the designated paretic side in a proposed simulation, NP the other side.
The designation is simulation metadata, never a predictor for a diagnostic model.

| Parameter | Definition / unit | Evidence status / allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| Walking speed | Forward progression, m/s; specify overground or relative to treadmill belt | Three published examples above. No universal severity bounds accepted. Lab-frame treadmill pelvis velocity is not walking speed. |
| Cadence | Steps/min, two alternating steps per cycle | Required explicit input. No clinical defaults adopted. |
| Step-length ratio | P step length / NP step length, dimensionless | Preserve which foot ends each step. Values above and below 1 permitted. Example observations only. |
| Contact phase phi | NP initial contact between consecutive P contacts, fraction of cycle | Modeling coordinate, not a paper-derived clinical range. Must be between 0 and 1. |
| P and NP stance fractions | Contact to ipsilateral final contact, divided by cycle duration | Required separately; each between 0 and 1. No clinical range accepted yet. |
| Step-time ratio | P step time / NP step time | Derived as (1-phi)/phi in this convention, not interchangeable with step-length ratio. |
| Swing / double support | Derived from the same periodic contact sequence | Not sampled independently of stance/contact timing. |
| Trunk compensation / foot clearance | Explicit kinematic coordinates, units, frames and measurement points required | Deferred pending evidence and a motion model. Not replaceable by arbitrary IMU gain/noise. |
| Severity, FAC, recovery | Separate clinical descriptors with their actual measurement scales | No deterministic mapping to parameter vectors or cross-sectional-to-longitudinal conversion accepted. |

Each future clinical bound needs source, cohort, task, assistance, measurement,
side convention, uncertainty and joint constraints. Unknown values remain unknown.
The paper's illustrative pairs do not supply cadence, timing and complete motion,
so they are not ready-made generator inputs.

## Implemented consistency checks

[derive_cycle](../../src/data/stroke_synthesis_contract.py) implements a steady,
forward, alternating, no-flight cycle. This is an engineering model assumption.
For cadence c and speed v, duration T=120/c and summed step lengths S=vT.
For ratio r, NP length=S/(1+r) and P length=rS/(1+r).

P contact occurs at phase 0, NP contact at phi, next P contact at 1. If P and NP
stance fractions are dP and dNP, no flight requires dP>=phi and dNP>=1-phi.
The two double-support fractions are phi+dNP-1 and dP-phi; their sum is
dP+dNP-1. Each swing duration is (1-d)T. Numerical validation rejects nonfinite,
nonpositive or inconsistent inputs; passing does not establish feasible joint
motion, ground reaction forces, foot clearance or a clinical stroke phenotype.

Five unit tests cover symmetric closure, both spatial asymmetry directions,
periodic stance overlap, flight-gap rejection and invalid inputs. Test parameters
are engineering fixtures, not clinical recommendations. Run:

## Executed rigid-attachment IMU pilot (2026-09-09)

Implemented [virtual_imu.py](../../src/data/virtual_imu.py) and executed
[the GAITEX experiment notebook](../../notebooks/35_virtual_imu_saved_results.ipynb).
This completes the ideal sensor calculation, not the healthy-to-stroke motion
transformation. Six new physics tests pass, alongside the five cycle tests.

For body-to-world rotation R, fixed sensor-to-body rotation A and body-fixed
offset d, sensor position is p+Rd and sensor orientation is RA. Specific force
is (RA)^T times (sensor acceleration minus world gravity). Angular velocity uses
centered local rotation increments, in radians/second. Rotation operations use
[SciPy's documented representation and composition API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation.html).
Inputs require finite, contiguous, uniform timestamps and proper rotations.
Tests cover gravity under attachment rotation, free fall, centripetal acceleration,
angular acceleration, world-frame invariance and malformed inputs. These are
numerical checks, not comparison with measured clinical IMU signals.

GAITEX execution uses the existing OpenSim-coordinate TRC files (metres converted
from verified mm headers, +Y vertical), rather than assuming raw CSV coordinates
match them. The origin is the first three pelvis markers' centroid. Marker edges
are explicitly orthogonalized into a right-handed frame. The attachment has zero
offset and identity orientation relative to that frame, NOT hardware IMU axes
and NOT L5. No anatomical attachment has been inferred.
