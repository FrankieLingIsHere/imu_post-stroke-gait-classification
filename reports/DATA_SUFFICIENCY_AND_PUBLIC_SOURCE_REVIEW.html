Data Sufficiency and Public Source Review

Gate 1 of the improvement sequence: decide what the present data can support before adding experiments or datasets.

Decision: The current data is enough to continue developing and benchmarking a research prototype. It is not enough for reliable age/sex subgroup claims or a clinical-ready stroke-specific model. The next data acquisition should target clinical specificity and metadata, not simply more windows from another incompatible source.

Current evidence sufficiency

Binary research prototypeYES — sufficient to proceed314 pooled participants; 126 healthy and 188 stroke; participant-disjoint internal validation plus RevalExo external testing.This supports continued model development and reproducible benchmarking.Do not inflate the evidence by counting windows as independent subjects.
Age-stratified stroke classifierNO — metadata gapAge bands exist for parts of the wider Voisard release, but current binary labels are not evenly represented: no CVA in the young band and limited healthy/stroke pairing by age.Run descriptive age coverage and age-confounding analysis only.Need linked individual age for both healthy and stroke groups in each intended band.
Sex-stratified performanceNO — incomplete linkageSome sources have sex information, but it is not consistently linked to the complete modeling/evaluation tables.Report available cohort summaries; do not claim fairness or parity.Need subject-level sex labels and adequate sample size per class/sex cell.
Stroke severity/generalisationPARTIALZhou adds 10 stroke participants with ages 37–88 and FAC 1–5, but no healthy controls and repeated rehabilitation visits.Use for auxiliary severity or longitudinal analysis with participant-level grouping.It should not be pooled as new binary healthy/stroke training data.
Stroke-specific clinical discriminationNO — control gapThe current healthy controls do not test whether the model confuses stroke with Parkinson’s, MS, fracture, COPD, or other mobility impairment.Treat current model as stroke-versus-healthy, not stroke-specific.Add non-stroke clinical cohorts for specificity testing.
Independent wearable-IMU validationPARTIALRevalExo provides a useful independent test, but only 17 participants.Keep it locked and report uncertainty.A second independent stroke+healthy wearable-IMU cohort would materially strengthen the claim.

Public-source shortlist

A source is not recommended for pooling merely because it contains gait data. Input channels, labels, participant independence, and clinical meaning must be compatible first.

SourceWhat it providesBest roleCompatibility limitationRecommended action
Mobilise-D CVS2400 participants across PD, MS, proximal femoral fracture, and COPD cohorts; processed back-worn digital mobility outcomes.High value for non-stroke clinical specificity and population robustness.Not a direct drop-in training source: current public release is processed DMO data rather than the same bilateral LB/LF/RF raw tensor format.Download a manageable subset after checking license, labels, and whether the released signals support a valid common representation.
Mobilise-D TVS / technical validationHealthy older adults plus PD, MS, COPD, fracture, and CHF-related cohorts; wearable sensor recordings and gold standards.Best conceptual match for clinical specificity and older-adult coverage.Channel placement and released format must be audited before pooling; it is not a stroke dataset.Prioritise as an exclusion/specificity evaluation set, not binary training by default.

Triaxial healthy reference60 healthy participants; ages 65–88.Useful older-healthy domain and age reference.No bilateral LB/LF/RF configuration, so direct classifier scoring would require invalid channel fabrication.Use for domain analysis or self-supervised pretraining only.
PiG / multimodal clinical gait referencesHealthy and stroke cohorts with motion capture, force, EMG, or related modalities.Useful for construct validity and clinical feature hypotheses.Not compatible with the current wearable-IMU input representation.Do not pool; use for interpretation and external construct comparison.

This review preserves the current pooled benchmark as the baseline. “Sufficient” means sufficient for the stated research claim, not sufficient for clinical deployment.
