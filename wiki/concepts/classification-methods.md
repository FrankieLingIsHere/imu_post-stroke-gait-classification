## The classical-to-deep-learning evolution

Classical machine learning dominates: **twelve of nineteen** included studies use a classical classifier or statistical method (SVM, random forest, decision tree, k-NN, logistic regression, an ensemble, or stepwise regression) — eleven exclusively, and one, [[rojek-2025]], alongside a deep learning classifier within the same study (see below). Individually, these twelve report accuracy from the mid-60s percent up to 100% on modest sample sizes, as few as 8 stroke participants in [[wang-2021]] and 14 in [[pohl-2022]] — [[obrien-2024]] (added 2026-07-28) sets the new floor at 65.9% for its hardest of three targets, discussed on its own terms just below rather than folded uncritically into the range, since it predicts functional discharge outcomes rather than a stroke-vs-comparison or severity-class label. **Corrected 2026-07-30**: a fourth `journal-critic` pass flagged that citing this as a single "65.9-100% accuracy" range, as the manuscript's Abstract and Conclusion previously did, contradicts Section 3.7's own statement that these figures are not directly comparable across tasks and populations. Both now state the figures individually rather than as a constructed range. Classical methods' feature-based design keeps the decision process interpretable — this is exactly what let the [[discriminative-features|age-confound check]] happen at all.

**Added 2026-07-28** via a fresh literature search: [[obrien-2024]], another classical-only (L1-penalized logistic regression) study, predicting three discharge functional outcomes rather than a diagnostic label — 84.4% accuracy for ambulation, 68.8% for independence, 65.9% for risk of falling, plus an honest negative result (sensor data did not improve prediction for the nonambulatory subgroup). This reinforces classical-method dominance further rather than narrowing it, and does not touch the classical-vs-deep-learning gap below, since it applies no deep learning method at all.

Six studies apply deep learning directly, not uniformly for classification:
- **Four** train a supervised classifier: [[wang-2021]] (97.31%, the highest among the deep-learning studies specifically, not the highest overall since [[lee-2018]]'s classical-method result reaches 100% on a comparably small sample, clean fixed bilateral-shank placement), [[rojek-2025]] (91.88% via CNN, comparably high, though its own text never confirms the sensor stream behind that figure), [[shin-2022]] (0.78 accuracy, noisy pocket placement), [[lee-2025]] (up to ~4% event-timing error, pocket placement, its own ~37-year unaddressed stroke/healthy age gap flagged in [[sensor-placement]]). The pocket-placed pair's lower headline figures suggest deep learning's raw-signal strength doesn't by itself overcome a harder input signal, though the studies' differing metrics keep this from a strictly quantified comparison — see [[placement-vs-practicality]].
- **One**, [[wu-2025]], trains a deep regression pipeline instead, reporting a severity-score correlation (r = 0.752) rather than a classification accuracy.
- **One**, [[felius-2024]], applies deep learning in a wholly unsupervised way (variational autoencoder) — no classification task, no accuracy figure at all.

Two, [[avvenuti-2018]] and [[ensink-2023]], use threshold-based signal processing with no trained classifier.

**Explicit verdict, added 2026-07-22**: Section 5.2 previously described this classical-vs-deep-learning pattern without stating a conclusion — the user asked directly whether a systematic review should go further than "here's what exists" toward "here's which approach is more plausible right now," given that's already the actual point of comparing prior works' performance in Table 3. Added: classical methods are the more practically plausible near-term choice, not because deep learning is weaker in principle, but because the current evidence doesn't yet support prioritizing it — deep learning's best result ([[wang-2021]]) depends on a clean, fixed placement, its worst results come from the noisier placements this review's own deployment interest actually targets, and 2 of its 5 studies ([[wu-2025]], [[felius-2024]]) don't attempt classification at all, leaving an even thinner evidence base than "5 studies" suggests. Deep learning reads as promising-but-unproven rather than lower-priority on technical merit, needing the method-matched comparison [[future-directions]] already recommends before it can be fairly judged.

## Further corrections, third journal-critic pass, 2026-07-29

A third adversarial review caught two more real issues in this same pooled comparison. First, "lower-back accelerometer RMS" was described as shared across all five healthy-reference datasets, but [[oxwalk]] (hip, wrist) and [[gaitmotion]] (foot only) have no lower-back sensor at all — the code substitutes each dataset's nearest trunk-proximal sensor as a proxy, a real methodological choice that was not disclosed to a manuscript reader. Now stated explicitly in Section 4.2.6. Second, that same pass claimed cadence and mean stride time are mathematically related *by definition* (asserting Table 6 defines stride time as exactly 120/cadence), collapsing the "five of six significant" headline to "four effectively independent quantities."

**Corrected 2026-07-30 (fourth `journal-critic` pass)**: that claim itself was wrong, and mattered enough to investigate directly rather than take on trust. A fourth adversarial review asked whether the divergent age-adjustment result (cadence loses significance under adjustment, stride time survives) could be an artifact of treating an exact reciprocal pair with independent linear residualization. Direct re-computation against the raw feature-engineering code settled it: for [[voisard-2025]] and [[felius-dataset]], stride time is measured independently from detected gait events, not derived from cadence at all, and the two correlate at Pearson r ≈ -0.60, roughly 10% off the idealized 120/cadence value in typical magnitude, not the exact identity the prior fix asserted. Table 6's own formula was corrected to state this accurately. Because the two features are related without being identical, this review no longer collapses them into "four effectively independent quantities" — they are reported as overlapping, non-fully-independent evidence instead, and the divergent age-adjustment finding is treated as genuine rather than an artifact. See [[discriminative-features]] for the full disclosure, including the genuine asymmetry that the pooled *healthy-reference* datasets, unlike Voisard/Felius, do compute stride time as exactly 120/cadence, since no independently labeled gait event exists for those signals.

**Also added 2026-07-30, same pass**: a per-healthy-dataset sensitivity check on the pooled comparison itself, since every stroke participant comes from only two datasets while every healthy participant comes from five different ones. Trunk RMS is not significant against OxWalk alone and cadence/stride time are not significant against DUO-GAIT alone, though both remain significant against the other four datasets individually. Excluding GaitMotion's Normal condition entirely leaves the result materially unchanged.

## Thirteenth journal-critic pass, 2026-07-31: two real code bugs, not disagreements, behind the "five of six" headline

A thirteenth adversarial review found the fourth pass's "r ≈ -0.60, roughly 10% off the idealized identity" correlation figure above, and the fifth pass's "cadence needs an age caveat" and "stride-time CV cancels between Voisard and Felius" conclusions, were all downstream of two real bugs in this review's own extraction code, not genuine findings. First, [[voisard-2025]]'s cadence denominator divided event counts by the recording's full length minus only the u-turn, silently counting idle recording time as walking time, more severely for faster walkers — this is what manufactured the apparent raw-cadence reversal the age-confound story explained; corrected, cadence is not an age artifact and survives age-adjustment as robustly as trunk RMS (r = 0.44). Second, stride-time mean and CV diffed gait-event timestamps across the u-turn boundary, splicing one artificial multi-second "stride" into every trial with a turn — corrected, Voisard's own cadence/stride-time correlation is now r ≈ -0.96 (not -0.60), nearly as tight as [[felius-dataset]]'s exact algebraic identity. A third, separate bug was found in `src/features/cross_dataset.py`'s own harmonic-ratio computation: it passed a step-rate frequency where the stride-rate fundamental was expected, for the three trunk-equivalent healthy-reference datasets computed via that path (DUO-GAIT, MAREA, Camargo). Fixed, harmonic ratio is **no longer significant** in the pooled comparison (r = 0.11, 95% CI crosses zero) and shows a genuine cross-dataset direction disagreement (positive against Camargo/DUO-GAIT/MAREA, negative against OxWalk) rather than the uniform significance the bug had produced. Stride-time variability's earlier "cancellation" story is also gone — both Voisard-alone and Felius-alone now agree in direction and magnitude against the pooled reference (r ≈ 0.46-0.49 each) — but this review does not read the pooled result as clean confirming evidence either, since the pooled healthy reference mixes two incompatible stride-time-CV estimators and one matched dataset (DUO-GAIT) returns an implausible outlier value even among datasets sharing the same estimator. **Trunk RMS, cadence, mean stride time, and sample entropy are now the four features this review treats as credibly generalizing from the pooled comparison**, with stride-time variability reported as unresolved rather than confirmed or failed, and harmonic ratio reported as not generalizing. A fourth new check this round: Voisard's CVA cohort is recorded 100% on one accelerometer platform while its HS cohort mixes two, a real hardware confound, but a device-matched sensitivity check found every affected effect size held or strengthened rather than weakened, evidence against the confound explaining the reported group differences. Full numbers, all with bootstrap 95% CIs, in [[discriminative-features]]. The `voisard.py` and `cross_dataset.py` fixes were verified against the two example trials the critic cited by hand, against the project's own re-executed analysis notebook, and cross-checked between native Windows Python and a WSL/Linux Python environment (needed to work around a local Application Control policy blocking the `matio` package used for Camargo) before being trusted.

## The unresolved comparison — narrowed, not closed, as of 2026-07-28, now reflected in the manuscript

Until 2026-07-28, no included study applied both a classical and a deep learning classifier to the identical sensor configuration, task, and population — the closest available contrast was placement-matched, not method-matched. ## Fourteenth journal-critic pass, 2026-07-31: the round-13 windowing fix was incomplete, and OxWalk had its own separate bug

A 14th adversarial review, dispatched only after `journal-format-check`, `academic-tone-check`, and `reviewer-neutrality-check` all ran clean first (a newly-hardened precondition in the global `journal-critic` agent definition), found round 13's cadence-denominator fix had not been extended to the review's other raw-signal features, and a second, independent bug in how OxWalk's RMS was computed. Both fixed in code and fully re-derived, not just patched in prose:

1. **Voisard's RMS/sample entropy/harmonic ratio/Poincare SD1 were still reading the whole raw file.** Round 13 fixed cadence's denominator to exclude idle padding and the u-turn, but `_channel_accel_rms` and `_channel_nonlinear_features` kept reading the entire `_raw_data_*.txt` file. Fixed by deriving explicit walking-only sample bounds from the same labeled-event data cadence already uses, and restricting every raw-signal feature to them.
2. **OxWalk's lower-back RMS was computed over an entire free-living day**, while `oxwalk.py`'s own docstring already restricts cadence and step-time CV to "active walking bouts... to avoid diluting cadence with long idle/non-walking stretches." Verified directly against a sample file: only ~13% of rows fall inside an active bout. Fixed by adding `oxwalk.bout_mask()` and applying it before computing RMS, matching the existing convention.

**The result is the biggest single improvement to this pooled comparison across all fourteen rounds.** Trunk RMS rises from r = 0.59 to **r = 0.88** [0.82, 0.92] and now separates the pooled stroke group from every one of the five healthy-reference datasets individually, with no exception — OxWalk, the one dataset it previously failed against, was explained at the time as a hip-vs-trunk placement effect; it was actually the OxWalk bug. Sample entropy flips from spuriously significant (contradicted by its own within-Felius null, a self-contradiction the manuscript itself had briefly carried between two adjacent paragraphs) to cleanly non-significant everywhere once the same windowing fix is applied. See [[discriminative-features]] for the full corrected numbers, the per-dataset trunk-RMS medians now disclosed directly in the manuscript (a real ~0.22g spread remains between the three genuine trunk-channel datasets, honestly flagged rather than smoothed away), and the explicit stride-time-variability sign-reversal disclosure this pass added.

**A fourth code bug, self-identified the same day while investigating this pass's own open item**: DUO-GAIT's sacral placement on symmetric healthy gait produces two comparably strong autocorrelation peaks, the true step period and the stride period exactly double it, and `dominant_stride_frequency` locked onto whichever was marginally stronger per trial — confirmed directly by inspecting several subjects' own autocorrelation arrays. Fixed via two opt-in additions to `signal_utils.py` (`dominant_stride_frequency`'s new `disambiguate_harmonic` parameter, and a matching outlier filter in `windowed_stride_time_cv` for the shorter, noisier 5 s windows), both defaulting off so no other dataset is affected. Cadence and mean stride time strengthen again (r = 0.57, r = -0.55) and now hold against every individual healthy dataset with no exception at all, closing the previously-disclosed "unexplained DUO-GAIT failure." Stride-time variability's status changes from "unresolved" to **resolved as a null finding**: restricted to the datasets sharing Voisard's/Felius's own windowed estimator, the effect vanishes (r = -0.03, p = .76); the full pool's nominal significance traces to OxWalk's own differently-defined step-time CV, four to five times every other dataset's value. (2018)'s placement count (four, not five, in one location), the SNR ranking's unstated raw-vs-age-adjusted basis, MAREA's undisclosed two-protocol composition in the pooled comparison, and a further, unsuccessful attempt at Camargo's landmark (now genuinely treated as unresolvable from available sources).
