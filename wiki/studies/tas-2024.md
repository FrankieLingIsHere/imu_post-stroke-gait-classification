Taş, H., Yardımcı, A., Uysal, H., & Bilge, U. (2024). *Classification of hemiplegia through gait analysis and machine learning methods*. *Marmara Medical Journal*, 37(1), 5–10. https://doi.org/10.5472/marumj.1379890. Verified via a real full-text fetch (DergiPark, open access). Found via the same IEEE Xplore/Scopus supplementary search as [[scheffer-2012]] and [[iosa-2021]]. One of three studies testing a trunk-region sensor alone (with [[iosa-2021]] and [[igarashi-2024]]), and the only included study whose classification target is Brunnstrom Stage specifically.

## Key findings

- Predicts Brunnstrom Stage (III-VI, hemiplegia severity) rather than a binary stroke-versus-healthy label, from wavelet-decomposed (Db5, level 6) single-accelerometer signal — one of the review's severity-grouping studies alongside Fugl-Meyer, discharge ambulation, fall-risk, and functional ambulation category examples (Section 4.1.3).
- Best accuracy: 91% (LogitBoost, WEKA) on the original 35-sample set, 90% on an expanded 50-sample set after adding 15 more healthy participants in a second data-collection phase — accuracy held up rather than degrading materially as the sample grew. RUSBoosted Trees (MATLAB) was the second-best algorithm at 86.1%.
- Only 7 healthy participants in the original comparison group (expanded to 22 in Part 2), among the smallest healthy-control arms of any included study — worth reading alongside [[quality-assessment]]'s control-group-matching finding below rather than treating the headline accuracy alone as the full picture.
- [[quality-assessment]]: participant-level 10-fold cross-validation confirmed (one sample per participant) — one of five of the six newly-assessed studies with a clean held-out/CV design. No code or data availability statement. Brunnstrom stage used explicitly as the severity measure, though time since stroke (chronicity) is not reported. Not tested for control-group comparability — patient age 67±11 versus the healthy group, no statistical check run.
