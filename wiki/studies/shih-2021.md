## Key findings

- **This review's only external, cross-system and cross-dataset generalization evidence, in either the classical or deep learning literature.** A DNN trained on IMU data (16 stroke vs. 9 healthy, 99.04% accuracy via k-fold CV) was then tested, without retraining, against two entirely different data sources: an optical motion-capture system on a separate cohort (10 stroke participants, 98.68% accuracy) and PAMAP2, an independent public dataset never intended for stroke-gait classification, distinguishing normal gaits at 98.25% accuracy. All three figures stay above 98%.
- Bilateral shank placement (APDM OPAL sensors) — one of three studies testing a shank placement alone, alongside [[orfanos-2026]], both grouped against Hsu et al.'s (2018) shank-leading literature result in Section 4.1.4.
- Consistent with [[classification-methods]]'s deep-learning input-quality-trade-off pattern: this is one of the two highest deep-learning accuracies, both on a clean, fixed placement (bilateral shank here; bilateral shank for [[wang-2021]] too), the pattern the review's own Conclusion (Section 8.3) contrasts against the noisier pocket-placement deep-learning studies' lower accuracy.
- [[quality-assessment]]: held-out/cross-validated evaluation confirmed (k=4-fold CV, plus the genuine cross-system and cross-dataset external testing above — a stronger validation design than most other included studies, which stop at CV alone). No code or data availability statement. Neither chronicity nor severity reported beyond "post-stroke patients." No control-group matching statement — per-subject demographics are tabulated but no comparability check is run.
