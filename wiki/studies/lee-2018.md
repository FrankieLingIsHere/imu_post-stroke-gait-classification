Lee, Park & Shin (2018), *Detection of hemiplegic walking using a wearable inertia sensing device*, *Sensors* 18(6):1736. Extracts the largest raw feature set of any included study (165 time-frequency parameters from a single lumbar IMU), reduced to 2–4 through sequential forward search.

## Key findings

- 100% accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity — the strongest reported figure of any classical-method study, but on a sample of only 20+20 participants. Read as a near-ceiling overfitting caveat rather than a stable population estimate, not a genuinely superior method.
- Corroborates [[sensor-placement]]'s lumbar/lower-back convergence alongside [[hsu-2021]], [[inui-2026]], and [[abdollahi-2024]] ([[mannini-2016]] was removed from this list 2026-07-29 — verified shank-dominant, not lumbar-placement evidence).
- [[quality-assessment]]: held-out/CV confirmed (4-fold CV plus held-out split), no code/data statement, neither chronicity nor standardized severity reported, healthy group screened by manual muscle test (functional screening only, not age-matching — no matching rationale stated).
