Hsu et al. (2021), *Can trunk acceleration differentiate stroke patient gait patterns using time- and frequency-domain features?*, *Applied Sciences* 11(4):1541. A single-lower-back-sensor study using SNR-based feature selection — the exact approach this review's own hands-on data mining adopts directly (see [[quality-assessment]] and the notebook's `feature_snr` function).

## Key findings

- 93.46% accuracy under 10-fold cross-validation on 18 training subjects (9 healthy, 9 stroke), 96.55% on a genuine held-out test of 5 unseen subjects (2 healthy, 3 stroke) across 6 trials each.
- Two-stage feature selection: ANOVA narrowed 42 time/frequency-domain features to 15 significant ones, then the SNR-style between-group/within-group variance ratio narrowed those to a final 4 (RMS and SD of vertical acceleration, band power at 99% bandwidth in the anteroposterior and vertical axes).
- The SNR-based feature-selection method is reused directly in this review's own hands-on mining, where `lb_sampen` ranked second only to `lb_accel_rms`.
- No data/code availability statement, no chronicity (time since stroke) reported, no severity score reported, and no explicit statistical check that the stroke group (63.4y) and the young-healthy subgroup (23.5y) are comparable — bare demographics only, the same pattern as several other included studies (see [[quality-assessment]]).

## Verification note (added 2026-07-29, corrects a stale claim)

Until 2026-07-29 this page and the manuscript both described this study as verified only against a search-engine-summarized abstract, since a direct fetch of the publisher's MDPI page returns HTTP 403. A `journal-critic` adversarial review flagged this as a fatal eligibility problem — the manuscript's own exclusion criterion EC2 excludes studies with an inaccessible full text, yet this study's placement-convergence and feature-ranking-method claims were being treated as fully included. Rather than excluding the study on that stale statement, a fresh retrieval was attempted via a text-extraction proxy route before deciding anything. That succeeded: the full methods and results above (participant breakdown, cross-validation design, exact feature-selection pipeline) all came from that retrieval, not from an abstract. The HTTP 403 on a direct fetch is MDPI blocking automated requests, not a real paywall — *Applied Sciences* is gold open access. The study stays included, and both this page and the manuscript's Table 4 / Section 6 have been corrected to reflect full-text verification rather than the earlier lower tier.
