Hsu et al. (2018), *Multiple-wearable-sensor-based gait classification and analysis in patients with neurological disorders*, *Sensors* 18(10):3397. The field's first systematic multi-placement comparison: five classical classifiers benchmarked separately at five body sites.

## Key findings

- Shank placement reaches the highest accuracy overall: 89.13% (decision tree), ahead of foot (82.61%) and thigh/lower back (80.43% each). An early, direct demonstration that placement choice alone moves accuracy by close to ten percentage points.
- Updated 2026-08-01: this result once read as a disagreement with [[sensor-placement]]'s own re-mined findings from [[voisard-2025]] and [[felius-2024]], but that framing was itself based on stale, pre-participant-level numbers from those two datasets. Corrected, [[voisard-2025]] and [[felius-2024]] both show foot RMS with a *larger* effect size than lower-back RMS, the same direction as Hsu's own shank/foot-ahead-of-lower-back ordering. All three sources now converge on distal placements matching or exceeding lower back, not disagreeing with it — the comparison target still differs (stroke-vs-other-neurological-disorder here, vs. stroke-vs-healthy there), so this is read as convergent rather than confirmed as the identical underlying effect, per the manuscript's own Section 5.1 treatment.
- [[quality-assessment]]: held-out/CV confirmed (5-fold CV plus held-out split), no code/data statement, neither chronicity nor severity reported, comparison group ("other neurological disorders") given bare demographics with no matching statement.
- The only one of 21 studies in [[jiao-2024]]'s review to avoid within-participant train/test leakage altogether — the field's weakest-criterion exception.
