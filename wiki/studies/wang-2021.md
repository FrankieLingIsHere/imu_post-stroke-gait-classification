Wang et al. (2021), *Detection and classification of stroke gaits by deep neural networks employing inertial measurement units*, *Sensors* 21(5):1864. The clearest example of the field's shift from hand-engineered features to end-to-end deep learning — raw six-axis signal straight into a two-stage DNN, no feature engineering step at all.

## Key findings

- 99.35% detection accuracy, 97.31% classification accuracy across four gait-abnormality types: drop foot, circumduction, hip hiking, back knee — the highest accuracy in the entire included set.
- One of only three studies in [[jiao-2024]]'s 21-study review to use multi-label classification, and the only one connecting output to specific joint deviations rather than group-level statistics.
- Achieves this on a clean, fixed bilateral-shank placement — see [[classification-methods]] for the placement-quality trade-off against the noisier, pocket-placed [[shin-2022]] and [[lee-2025]].
- [[quality-assessment]]: held-out/CV confirmed (4-fold), dataset link provided (no code link), severity reported via Brunnstrom stage/FAC/MMSE but no chronicity, healthy group age 23–26 vs stroke 48–72 with no matching statement despite the large gap.
