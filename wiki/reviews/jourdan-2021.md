Jourdan, T., Debs, N., & Frindel, C. (2021), systematic review of machine learning's contribution to validating commercial wearable sensors for gait monitoring across patient populations generally (not stroke-specific). The widest-angle view among the five prior reviews.

## What it covers

70 studies, 2010–2020. Stroke's own share of this landscape is small: vascular disease (the category stroke falls under) accounts for only 24% of included studies (18/70), similar to orthopedic disorders and behind neurodegenerative disease (33%). Machine learning of any kind used in only 17% of studies (12/70) — a striking contrast with how dominant classical ML is within the smaller, stroke-specific literature [[jiao-2024]] reviews.

## Placement finding

Inferior placement at leg/foot dominates (60%), chest/trunk appears in 49%, free pocket-carried placement in only 17% — despite this review's own [[placement-vs-practicality]] evidence that pocket placement already works in a real stroke population.

## Links

Its own conclusion — that commercial sensors combining patient adherence with miniaturization and low power will be central to the field's future, and that chronic-pathology-specific validation protocols are still needed — is echoed almost exactly by this review's own positioning.
