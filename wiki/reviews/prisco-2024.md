Prisco, G. et al. (2024), *Validity of wearable inertial sensors for gait analysis: A systematic review*, *Diagnostics* 15(1):36. Asks the same validity question as [[da-silva-2024]] at a much wider population scope — pathologies generally, not stroke specifically.

## What it covers

32 studies, 2012–2023. Only 2 of 32 involved stroke patients — most cover Parkinson's disease (5 studies), a scatter of single-study conditions (transfemoral amputation, obesity, HIV, MS, PSP, idiopathic toe walking), and a majority in healthy participants.

## The placement convergence

Lower back is already the field's default even outside stroke research: the most common single-sensor configuration (22/32 studies), most frequent multi-sensor combination (lower back + shanks + feet + thighs, 10 studies) — directly corroborating [[sensor-placement]]'s finding from an entirely independent literature base. Measurement accuracy against optical mocap: good for cadence/stride time/gait cycle time, more variable for swing/stance-phase parameters.

Its own stated limitation — heterogeneity of sensor configurations, processing methods, and validation metrics precluding a pooled meta-analysis — is the same heterogeneity problem this review itself cites for why it can't run a meta-analysis on its own 17 included studies.
