Boukhennoufa, I. et al. (2022), *Wearable sensors and machine learning in post-stroke rehabilitation assessment: A systematic review*, *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control* 71:103197. Widens the lens from measurement validity to system design broadly.

## What it covers

33 publications on wearable sensors and ML in post-stroke rehabilitation assessment, organized into three roughly equal groups: activity recognition (11), movement classification (12), clinical assessment emulation (10).

## Key patterns

Upper-limb systems mostly use wrist (12) and arm (10); lower-limb systems mostly use chest (8) then shank (4). SVM most-used overall; MLP most common neural-network architecture; k-NN in only 3 of 33 studies. Performance ranges widely (~70–99%) depending on activity and configuration.

Small sample size is the single most frequently cited limitation. Failing to age-match able-bodied controls to the stroke cohort is flagged as a specific issue in two included studies — this review's own hands-on data mining independently rediscovers the same problem on [[voisard-2025]] (see [[discriminative-features]]).
